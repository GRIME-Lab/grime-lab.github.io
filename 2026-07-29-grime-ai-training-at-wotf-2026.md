*By John E. Stranzl Jr., PhD*

Today I presented a hands-on GRIME AI training at the [2026 Water Observing Technology Forum](https://ciroh.ua.edu/water-observing-technology-forum/) (WOTF) in Tuscaloosa, Alabama. Troy Gilmore attended and provided support during the session.

## About the forum

WOTF is hosted by the Cooperative Institute for Research to Operations in Hydrology (CIROH) at the University of Alabama. The planning committee also includes the Alabama Water Institute, the USGS Next Generation Water Observing System and Hydrologic Instrumentation Facility, NOAA, and the Alliance for Coastal Technologies.

This year's theme was non-contact water monitoring technologies enhanced with artificial intelligence. The forum ran July 28 to 30. The second day was held at the USGS Hydrologic Instrumentation Facility (HIF) and was devoted to hands-on training workshops, including a track on image-based monitoring.

## The training

The session, **"GRIME-AI: Boosting Environmental Monitoring Using Camera Networks,"** walked through the full GRIME AI workflow for turning time-lapse imagery from camera networks into water-related information:

- **Image acquisition** from public camera networks, including USGS gauging-station cameras, PhenoCam, and NEON, as well as trail cameras.
- **Data cleaning and management** to sort and organize large image sets before analysis.
- **Image annotation** to build labeled training data for segmentation.
- **Fine-tuning segmentation models** on that labeled data.

The demonstrations used imagery from USGS gauging stations, so participants could see each step run on real monitoring data.
